Buyer playbook (2026 edition)

  1. Bulk import beat direct mail. With de minimis gone (US, Aug 2025) and the EU's €150 exemption ending Jul 2026, sea-freight bulk into a domestic warehouse is the structural winner. Price everything at landed cost, not EXW.
  2. Ask for FOB, not EXW. Under EXW you pay inland China trucking and export clearance — the classic first-order surprise. Small buyers should insist on FOB quotes.
  3. Certify before the PO, not after. Lab testing (CE/FCC/EN71/ASTM) takes 2–6 weeks and $300–3,000. Amazon now suppresses toy listings without annual tests. Start testing with your sample order.
  4. Badges aren't vetting. "Gold Supplier" is a paid membership tier. Third-party factory audits and pre-shipment inspection ($200–350/day) are the only real assurance — 10–15% of FBA-bound shipments contain defects that trigger returns.
  5. Pay 30/70, inspect before the 70. Deposit 30%, balance after passed pre-shipment inspection against a written spec sheet with AQL sampling.
  6. Respect the calendar. Summer SKUs are ordered by February, Q4 gifting SKUs by August. Each card shows its order-by month — miss it and you're funding next year's inventory.
  7. Saturated ≠ forbidden, but demands an angle. Red badges mean you need a bundle, spec upgrade, accessory play, or an under-served market (often the EU) — never a price war.
  8. Own a consumable backend. Filters, brush heads, tea refills, paper rolls: the picks with repeat-purchase attachments compound; one-shot products don't.
